Shop Drawings vs Construction Drawings: What's the Difference?

Arcview Studio Team Jun 12, 2026 5 min read Shop Drawings

On almost every project we take on, someone asks the same question at some point: aren't shop drawings and construction drawings basically the same thing? They come from the same design intent and often look similar at first glance, but the level of detail — and who actually uses each set on site — is very different. Getting this distinction right early saves real time once fabrication starts.

What Are Construction Drawings?

Construction drawings are prepared by the architect or structural engineer to communicate design intent, layout, dimensions and code compliance. They're what gets submitted to authorities for approval and what contractors use to price and plan the overall build. They tell you what is being built and roughly where, but they rarely tell a fabricator exactly how a joint, bracket or panel should be cut and assembled.

What Are Shop Drawings?

Shop drawings take that design intent and translate it into fabrication-ready instructions. They're typically prepared by the contractor, fabricator or a specialist detailing studio like ours, and they include the dimensions, materials, hardware, tolerances and assembly sequence needed to actually manufacture and install the element — whether that's a joinery unit, a steel connection or an MEP duct run.

"A construction drawing tells you what to build. A shop drawing tells the workshop exactly how to build it — down to the last screw."

Key Differences at a Glance

  • Author: Construction drawings come from the architect/engineer; shop drawings come from the contractor or fabricator.
  • Purpose: Construction drawings secure approvals; shop drawings drive fabrication and installation.
  • Detail level: Shop drawings include hardware call-outs, tolerances, material grades and assembly sequence that construction drawings typically omit.
  • Audience: Construction drawings are read by approval authorities and site teams; shop drawings are read by workshop and installation crews.
  • Timing: Shop drawings are produced after construction drawings are approved, and often go through their own review/approval cycle before fabrication starts.

Why This Distinction Matters for Your Timeline

Projects that skip proper shop drawing development — and try to fabricate straight from construction drawings — almost always run into avoidable site issues: hardware that doesn't fit, panel sizes that clash with structural tolerances, or MEP runs that collide with joinery. Every one of those becomes a site query, a fabrication delay, or expensive rework. A dedicated shop drawing stage catches these problems on paper, where they're cheap to fix, instead of on site, where they aren't.
